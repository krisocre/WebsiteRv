Google review removal cost in Canada: what businesses actually pay

Google’s own reporting tools are free. A professional fee pays for the work around the report: assessment, evidence organization, submission management and follow-up. Here is how to compare the price, the process and the result before hiring anyone.

By ReviewsBoost Editorial Team Published September 13, 2026 Updated September 13, 2026 10-minute read
ReviewsBoost published price: $30 CAD upfront per review, plus $40 CAD only after that review is removed. One successfully removed review therefore costs $70 CAD in total. Sending the initial request form does not take payment.

How much does it cost to remove a Google review?

There are two different costs people often mix together. Google charges $0 to report a review through a Business Profile or its Reviews Management Tool. A third-party Google review removal service may charge for the time and specialist work used to assess the review, organize evidence, manage the report and follow up.

ReviewsBoost uses a two-part price: $30 CAD upfront for each review submitted and a $40 CAD success fee for each review removed. If a submitted review is not removed, the $40 success fee for that review is not charged. The written service terms should identify when the upfront fee is due and how a successful result is recorded.

What a professional review removal fee should cover

A legitimate fee should correspond to defined work. Ask the provider to put the scope in writing so you can compare more than the headline number. A managed service may include:

  • reviewing the review text, rating, profile and surrounding facts;
  • matching the concern to the most relevant current policy category;
  • organizing screenshots, URLs, dates and supporting evidence;
  • managing the initial submission and recording its status;
  • preparing a concise appeal where Google makes that option available;
  • providing status updates and confirming whether the review remains visible.

Google says a reported review is evaluated against its policies and that a “no policy violation” decision may have a one-time appeal option. This is why documentation and a precise explanation matter. The current process is described in Google Business Profile Help.

Professional review removal vs reporting it yourself

Reporting through Google can be done without paying Google. The practical choice is whether your business wants to perform and track the work internally or pay a specialist to manage it. The platform’s decision remains independent either way.

If you choose the direct route, use the official Google Reviews Management Tool. If you want the process managed, compare providers using the checklist below rather than choosing solely by price.

How to choose a review removal company in Canada

The best Google review removal service for a particular business is the one that clearly explains its scope, price, evidence and limitations before payment. A low initial number can be misleading if the provider does not define later fees. A large success claim can also be misleading if it has no denominator, time period or definition.

Ask these eight questions before hiring

  1. What is the complete price in CAD? Separate upfront, per-review, success, tax and exceptional charges.
  2. What exactly counts as success? Ask whether the review must stop appearing publicly and how that is verified.
  3. What work begins after the upfront payment? The answer should name concrete deliverables.
  4. Who makes the final decision? A credible provider should explain the platform’s role accurately.
  5. How was the success rate calculated? Request the numerator, denominator, tracking period and exclusions.
  6. What happens if the review remains live? Understand success-fee, cancellation and refund terms.
  7. Will you need my password? Do not share a Google password. If account access is ever needed, Google provides separate owner and manager roles.
  8. How will my information be handled? Look for a privacy policy, limited data collection and a clear contact route.
Warning signs: an unexplained “guarantee,” payment requested through an untraceable method, pressure to act immediately, vague claims about secret access to Google, no written terms, no privacy policy, or a request for your Google password.

What makes a Google review removal service legitimate?

A legitimate Google review removal company should identify itself, publish contact and policy pages, describe the service it actually performs and avoid implying that it owns or controls Google’s platform. It should also distinguish an internal service result from a universal prediction.

Google’s Maps policy says reviews should reflect genuine, unbiased experiences and lists fake engagement, conflicts of interest, harassment, personal information, off-topic material and other prohibited content. A provider should work from those published categories rather than claim that every negative opinion has the same basis. Read the Google review policy guide for Canadian businesses or Google’s current prohibited and restricted content policy.

Canadian businesses should also take marketing claims seriously. The Competition Bureau has warned that fake reviews and disguised commercial representations can mislead consumers. A provider’s testimonials, customer totals and success claims should therefore be supportable. Google review removal confidence: what to look for

Strong removal confidence comes from careful assessment, clear case preparation and consistent follow-through. Look for a provider that explains who reviews the submission, how the removal work is managed and when progress updates are provided.

ReviewsBoost reports an 80% removal success rate. Confidence is supported by specialist assessment, focused preparation, managed removal work and direct status updates. The result-based portion of the fee follows removal, keeping the service focused on the outcome the client wants.

Use this four-part confidence check

  • Assessment: Is the review examined by a specialist?
  • Preparation: Is the case organized before removal work begins?
  • Follow-through: Does the provider manage the process from start to finish?
  • Updates: Will you receive clear progress information?

For broader context, platform moderation statistics do not predict an individual outcome. Our fake review statistics dataset keeps platform-specific definitions separate so removed, blocked and fraudulent content are not treated as the same measurement.

Does a higher price mean faster removal?

Price alone does not establish speed. Google says review evaluation typically takes several days, while interface availability and appeal timing can change. A provider can control how promptly it assesses, prepares and follows up on a file; it cannot set Google’s review queue. Ask for the provider’s update schedule and the date at which it will reassess the file.

Google review removal pricing FAQ

Is Google review reporting free?

Yes. Google’s official reporting route does not carry a Google reporting charge. Professional review removal pricing pays the service provider for its own work.

What is the maximum ReviewsBoost fee for one removed Google review?

Under the published $30 upfront plus $40 after-removal model, the service fee totals $70 CAD for one review that is successfully removed. Applicable taxes should be confirmed in the written checkout or service agreement.

Do I pay the $40 fee if the review is not removed?

No. The $40 CAD portion is described as an after-removal success fee. Read the current refund and outcome terms before authorizing the service.

Can a provider remove several reviews at once?

Several reviews can be assessed and tracked in the same service period, but each should have its own URL, evidence and outcome record. ReviewsBoost calculates the stated upfront and success fees per review.

Where can I request an exact total?

Choose the number of reviews in the Google review removal request section. The form collects the public business and review details needed for an initial assessment and does not take payment when submitted.
