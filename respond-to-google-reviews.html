Review response playbook

How to respond to Google reviews: frameworks and 30 examples

A useful response shows that a person read the feedback, protects customer privacy, and gives the next reader confidence in how your business handles praise and problems.

By ReviewsBoost Editorial TeamPublished September 2, 202614-minute read

Review replies are public customer service. The original reviewer matters, but so do future customers who may read the exchange months later. A calm, specific and proportionate reply usually builds more trust than a defensive explanation or a generic “Thanks for your feedback.”

A four-part response framework

  1. Acknowledge: greet the reviewer and recognize the feedback.
  2. Reflect: mention one safe, non-sensitive detail so the reply is clearly relevant.
  3. Respond: thank them, explain the improvement, or apologize for the experience without arguing.
  4. Route: if account details are needed, invite a private conversation through a real contact channel.
Keep private information private. Do not confirm that someone is a customer, patient, tenant or client when that relationship is sensitive. Do not post appointment details, invoice facts, diagnoses, legal matters, full names, phone numbers or internal notes. Move verification to a private channel.

How quickly should a business reply?

Consistency matters more than racing to reply in minutes. Establish a daily or several-times-weekly queue, with urgent escalation for threats, personal information, safety allegations and suspected fake reviews. Give an upset team member time to cool down before publishing.

How long should a reply be?

Match the depth of the review. A one-sentence positive rating may need two concise sentences. A detailed service failure deserves a more thoughtful response, but public replies should not become case files. Aim to resolve specifics privately.

Positive Google review response examples

1. Short five-star review

Thank you, [First Name]. We appreciate you choosing [Business Name] and taking a moment to leave a review. We hope to see you again.

2. Review mentioning an employee

Thank you for recognizing [Employee First Name]. We’ll make sure your kind feedback reaches them. We’re glad the team made your visit easy.

3. Repeat customer

Thank you for continuing to trust our team, [First Name]. It means a lot to hear that your experiences have been consistently positive.

4. First-time visitor

We’re glad your first visit went smoothly. Thank you for sharing what stood out, and welcome to [Business Name].

5. Detailed product praise

Thank you for the thoughtful review. We’re pleased that [safe product or service detail] worked well for you, and we appreciate the specific feedback.

6. Fast service

Thanks for highlighting the quick turnaround. Our team works hard to keep the process efficient without compromising the result.

7. Local business support

Thank you for supporting a Canadian business and for sharing your experience. We’re grateful to serve customers in [community].

8. Positive rating without text

Thank you for the rating, [First Name]. We appreciate your support.

Three- and four-star review response examples

9. Mostly positive with one suggestion

Thank you for the balanced feedback. We’re glad you enjoyed [positive detail], and your note about [improvement area] has been shared with the team.

10. Four stars with no comment

Thank you for your review. We appreciate the feedback and would be glad to learn what could make a future experience even better at [contact channel].

11. Wait-time concern

Thank you for your patience and for mentioning the wait. We’re reviewing how we schedule and communicate during busy periods so expectations are clearer.

12. Price concern in an otherwise positive review

Thank you for sharing both what worked and your thoughts on value. We aim to make pricing and what is included clear before service, and we’ll review that communication.

13. Product availability

We appreciate the feedback. We’re sorry your preferred item was unavailable, and we’ve passed the note to the team responsible for inventory.

Negative Google review response examples

Do not demand that the reviewer delete or change the post. Avoid blaming the customer, naming staff who were not already named, or asserting facts you cannot safely prove in public. If your business made a mistake, a direct apology is stronger than “we’re sorry you feel that way.”

14. General one-star review

We’re sorry this experience fell short. We’d like to understand what happened and identify a practical next step. Please contact [name/team] at [email or phone] with the date of your visit.

15. Service delay

We apologize for the delay and the lack of clear updates. That is not the communication standard we aim for. Please reach us at [contact] so we can review your visit privately.

16. Staff conduct complaint

Thank you for bringing this to our attention. We take concerns about how people are treated seriously. We’d like to gather the details privately at [contact] and review them with the appropriate manager.

17. Billing disagreement

We understand why an unexpected charge would be frustrating. We cannot review account details publicly, but our billing team can examine the invoice with you at [contact].

18. Quality problem

We’re sorry the result did not meet expectations. Thank you for describing the issue. Please contact [team] at [contact] so we can understand the circumstances and discuss available options.

19. Missed appointment or delivery

We apologize for the inconvenience and understand the time this cost you. Please send the booking or order details to [contact] so we can investigate without sharing personal information here.

20. Policy or refund dispute

We’re sorry the policy and available options were not clear. We’d like to review what was communicated and make sure you receive a complete explanation. Please contact [manager/contact].

21. Food or safety concern

Thank you for raising this. We take safety concerns seriously and are reviewing the matter. Please contact [manager] at [contact] with the visit details so we can investigate promptly.

22. Review written in anger

We can see that this experience was frustrating. We want to understand the underlying issue and keep the conversation constructive. Please contact [team] at [contact] so we can review it directly.

Inaccurate, mistaken or suspected fake review examples

Before stating that a review is fake, check alternate names, gift recipients, third-party bookings and dates. A reviewer may have interacted with the business without appearing under the public profile name. If the content appears to violate policy, preserve evidence and use the reporting process described in our fake Google review reporting guide.

23. No matching record

We have not been able to match the details in this review to our records. We still want to understand the concern. Please contact [team] at [contact] with the date and service location so we can investigate privately.

24. Wrong location

We’re sorry to hear about the experience described. The details do not appear to match this location. Please contact us at [contact] so we can help identify the correct business or review the matter.

25. Factually disputed account

We take this concern seriously, but our records differ from parts of the account posted here. To protect everyone’s privacy, we won’t discuss transaction details publicly. [Manager] is available at [contact] to review them directly.

26. Competitor or conflict-of-interest concern

We believe this post may involve a conflict of interest and have asked the platform to review it. If the author had a genuine customer experience, they can contact [team] at [contact] so we can investigate.

Sensitive-industry response examples

27. Healthcare

Thank you for sharing your concern. We protect the privacy of everyone who contacts our clinic and cannot discuss individual circumstances publicly. Our privacy contact can be reached at [contact].

28. Legal or financial service

We appreciate the feedback. Confidentiality prevents us from confirming or discussing any matter here. Anyone with a service concern can contact [role] at [contact] for a private review.

29. Housing or employment

Thank you for raising the concern. We will not discuss personal records or individual circumstances publicly. Please use [formal contact or complaint channel] so the matter can be reviewed appropriately.

30. Review involving a minor

We take concerns involving young people seriously and will not discuss any details publicly. Please contact [designated role] at [contact] so the appropriate person can review the matter privately.

A reliable response workflow

  1. Assign ownership. Decide who replies and who approves high-risk responses.
  2. Classify. Mark feedback positive, constructive, service recovery, suspected policy violation, privacy-sensitive or urgent.
  3. Verify privately. Check facts without publishing customer data.
  4. Draft for the next reader. Be calm, relevant and clear about the next step.
  5. Escalate when needed. Threats, personal data, discrimination allegations and safety reports need a defined path.
  6. Record the theme. A reply closes the public loop; an operational action prevents recurrence.

Should businesses use the same template every time?

Templates reduce delay and help maintain standards, but repeated exact wording looks inattentive. Treat examples as structures. Change the opening, reflect a safe detail, and vary the close. Never invent a detail merely to sound personalized.

Should a business mention keywords in review replies?

Write for the reviewer, not an imagined algorithm. It is natural to use the business name or service when relevant, but forced location-and-service phrases make replies awkward and can reduce trust. Accurate, useful language is the better standard.

Can I ask a reviewer to update a review after resolving the issue?

Focus first on resolving the concern. Do not offer an incentive or condition service on a change. A neutral note that the customer is free to update their review based on the full experience should only come after resolution and without pressure.

Pre-publish checklist

  • The response addresses the correct review and location.
  • The tone is calm and would make sense to a future customer.
  • No private or sensitive information is confirmed.
  • No unsupported accusation or legal threat appears.
  • The next step uses a monitored email or phone number.
  • The reply is proportional and avoids a public argument.
  • A serious issue has an internal owner beyond the public reply.
