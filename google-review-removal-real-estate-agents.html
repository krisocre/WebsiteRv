When a referral turns into a Google search

A friend recommends your team to a seller. The seller searches your name and sees a review claiming your brokerage charged a fee for a service you have never offered. They have two other agents to call. You may never know this seller considered you, which makes the loss easy to miss in a report on signed listings.

The Real Estate Council of Ontario advises buyers and sellers to ask for referrals, interview several agents and check references. The Google profile is another place a prospect can check your name between getting a recommendation and booking that interview. Clear service information and accurate profile details help a prospect decide whether to call. RECO’s guidance on choosing an agent.

Check whether the review describes your business

  • Wrong brokerage or office. Record the profile, review date and any public clues that point to a different business, such as an office location or another brokerage’s name.
  • Possible conflict of interest. If you can substantiate a reviewer’s professional connection to a competing business or your own brokerage, document the connection and how it relates to the review.
  • Real interactions without a closing. A seller may have attended a consultation or a buyer may have toured a home with your team without completing a sale. Check the interaction before treating a missing transaction as evidence.

A client’s criticism of your communication, fees or negotiation can be genuine feedback. Discuss specific complaints through your brokerage’s private process. For a possible violation, save the review link and a screenshot. Leave out client names, offer terms and private records when you first contact ReviewsBoost. Google Maps content policies.

Work out what one extra opportunity is worth

Use your own numbers. In a hypothetical closing, a team collects CAD $4,000 after its brokerage split and spends CAD $700 on expenses tied to that listing. That leaves CAD $3,300 before overhead and tax. The calculation starts after closing, so compare it with how often a listing consultation actually becomes a closed transaction.

For a month, record how new callers heard about you, how many book a consultation, and how many sign. Ask separately whether they checked your profile. A caller can be both a referral and a Google profile visitor. Tracking only the first source hides part of the decision.
