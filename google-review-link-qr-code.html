Review request setup

How to create and use a Google review link and QR code

A direct link removes unnecessary steps. The rest of the job is testing it, matching it to the correct location, placing it after a genuine experience, and making the invitation accessible.

By ReviewsBoost Editorial TeamPublished September 2, 20269-minute read

How to find your Google review link

Google Business Profile interfaces change, but the current owner workflow generally starts while signed in to the Google account that manages the profile. Search for the business, open the profile management controls, and look for an option such as “Ask for reviews” or “Get more reviews.” Copy the supplied link.

  1. Confirm you are managing the correct business and location.
  2. Open the review-request or ask-for-reviews control.
  3. Copy the official link without editing its parameters.
  4. Paste it into a private browser window.
  5. Confirm it opens the intended profile and review composer.

For multi-location brands, keep a simple location table containing location name, store number, city, Business Profile URL, review link, owner and last-tested date. A wrong-location link creates confusing feedback and can be difficult to correct later.

Do not copy a competitor’s format and replace an ID. Use the link provided from your own verified Business Profile controls. Place IDs, URLs and interface behaviour can change.

The review-link test checklist

  • Open the link while signed out or in a private window.
  • Test on a recent iPhone and Android device.
  • Test from both email and SMS, because some apps wrap links.
  • Confirm the exact business name, city and location.
  • Check that an existing customer can choose any rating and write their own words.
  • Confirm no survey or sentiment screen blocks people from the Google link.
  • Retest quarterly and after profile ownership, name or location changes.

Should you shorten a Google review link?

A branded short URL can make print and SMS cleaner, but it adds a redirect that you control. Use an HTTPS URL on your own domain when possible, keep the destination transparent, and monitor it so it does not expire. Avoid unfamiliar public shorteners that may look suspicious or trigger message filtering.

How to make a review QR code

  1. Start with the tested review link or your stable branded redirect.
  2. Generate a high-resolution SVG or print-quality PNG with a reputable QR tool.
  3. Use dark code modules on a plain light background.
  4. Keep the blank “quiet zone” around the code.
  5. Add a plain-language instruction and readable fallback URL.
  6. Print a draft at final size and scan it from normal distance.
  7. Test under the lighting and surface conditions where it will be used.

There is no one perfect printed size because scanning distance, camera, print quality and material differ. As a practical starting point for a card viewed at arm’s length, test at roughly 2.5–3 cm square or larger. Signs viewed from farther away need a larger code. Testing the actual output is more reliable than relying on a universal size rule.

Accessible caption

Share an honest Google review about your experience.
Scan the QR code or visit example.ca/review.
Need another way to provide feedback? Email service@example.ca.

Do not place critical explanatory text inside the QR graphic. Screen readers cannot interpret a printed code, and not everyone can scan it. The fallback URL and alternate feedback channel make the request more inclusive.

Can a QR code go on a business card?

Yes, but space is limited. Give it enough room, preserve the quiet zone, add a clear label, and print a fallback URL. A dedicated review card is often easier to understand than crowding a general business card.

Can a QR code be displayed before payment?

It can, but a review invitation makes more sense after the customer can evaluate the full experience. A post-service receipt, follow-up or exit placement usually provides better context.

Review-link request templates

Email

Subject: Share feedback about your visit to [Business Name]

Hi [First Name], thank you for visiting [location] on [date]. If you would like to share an honest review of your experience, use this link: [review link]. Your feedback helps our team improve and helps others make informed choices.

SMS

[Business Name]: Thank you for your visit. You can leave an honest Google review here: [short link]. Reply STOP to opt out.

Invoice footer

How did we do? Share an honest Google review at [short URL]. Feedback is optional and no incentive is offered.

QR sign

Visited us today? Share an honest review. Scan the code or visit [short URL].

Do not ask for “five stars,” prefill suggested praise or offer a discount, contest entry, gift or other benefit. Read the Google review policy guide for Canadian businesses before launch.

Can you track review-link and QR-code performance?

You can track visits to a branded redirect or landing page you control, and many QR platforms report aggregate scans. That data shows link use—not who posted, what rating they selected, or whether a scan became a review. Avoid collecting more personal data than needed.

For channel comparison, create transparent paths such as example.ca/review-receipt and example.ca/review-email that redirect to the same official link. Document the redirect owner and destination. Do not add tracking that reveals personal information in a public URL.

What is review gating?

Review gating occurs when a business screens sentiment and provides the public review path mainly to satisfied customers while directing dissatisfied customers somewhere else. A common example is a survey asking “Were you happy?” and showing the Google link only after a positive answer. Instead, make private support available to everyone and make the same honest public-review invitation available to all eligible customers.

Multi-location rollout checklist

  • Every location has a distinct, verified review link.
  • Store numbers and city names are in the master mapping.
  • QR artwork includes a location label.
  • Short redirects have permanent owners and documented destinations.
  • Managers test the code before printing and quarterly afterward.
  • Staff use neutral, approved language.
  • Reporting compares locations fairly and accounts for invitation volume.
