Glassdoor eligibility

Bad reviews require a specific policy reason

Critical opinions about a genuine workplace experience normally remain when they follow Glassdoor’s rules, even if the employer strongly disagrees.

Policy signal

Fraud or inauthentic employment

Reliable evidence indicates the writer did not have a qualifying work relationship or misrepresented material facts.

Policy signal

Privacy or confidential information

The content improperly exposes personal data or material protected by applicable rights and policies.

Policy signal

Harassment, threats or abuse

The review contains targeted harmful conduct beyond ordinary workplace criticism.

Policy signal

Irrelevant or non-employment content

The post is unrelated to workplace experience or belongs to another employer profile.

A low score is not proof of a violation. We screen the policy basis before accepting a case and do not promise that criticism will be removed.

Questions

Glassdoor review removal FAQ

Can every bad Glassdoor review be removed?

No. Glassdoor makes the final decision. A review needs a credible policy violation; a negative opinion alone is not sufficient.

How much does Glassdoor review removal cost?

Each accepted review costs $30 CAD upfront. The $95 CAD success fee applies only if that review is removed.

Why is the success fee different by platform?

Each platform uses different rules, evidence expectations and follow-up paths. Glassdoor is classified as high complexity work, so its success fee reflects the case effort required.

What should I send first?

Send the Glassdoor business or employer profile URL, the review link or screenshots, the reason it may violate policy and dated supporting records. Do not send passwords.

What happens if the review stays online?

The success fee is not charged for that review. The upfront fee covers assessment and case preparation under the refund and service terms.

Can you submit a response instead?

Yes. When a review appears eligible to remain, a careful public response and internal recovery plan may be more useful than repeated reporting.
