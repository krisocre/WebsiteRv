Google reviews not showing? Causes, checks and next steps

A missing review is not automatically deleted or fake. It may be undergoing a policy check, affected by a recent profile change, removed for a violation or temporarily unavailable. Diagnose the pattern before contacting support or asking a customer to act.

By ReviewsBoost Editorial Team Reviewed September 3, 2026 General information for Canadian businesses
Quick answer

Google says review checks can delay publication for a few days. Profile merges can also take a few days to settle. If the delay continues, identify whether one review, several reviews or the entire profile is affected, preserve the facts, check policy and then use official Google Business Profile support.

First identify the missing-review pattern

Different symptoms point to different next steps. Record what is actually happening before changing the profile or sending another request. Check the public profile in both Google Search and Maps, and compare the visible review count with any email notifications or screenshots you legitimately hold.

What you see Useful first check
One new review has not appeared Allow time for policy checks and confirm the reviewer used an updated Maps app.
Several older reviews disappeared together Check for a recent reinstatement, merge, category change or platform restriction.
The reviews appear but the count or rating looks stale Capture the displayed count in Search and Maps and recheck after normal processing time.
Reviews from two profiles have not combined Confirm the profiles were actually merged and allow a few days for the display to update.
No one can leave a review Check whether user-created content is restricted for the profile or business category.

Why a Google review may be delayed or missing

1. The review is still being checked

Google checks reviews against its policies. The company states that this process can sometimes take a few days. A review being visible to its author but not yet visible publicly does not prove what the final outcome will be.

2. Google removed it under a content policy

Reviews can be removed for spam, fake engagement, harassment, conflicts of interest, off-topic content and other prohibited or restricted material. Google says reviews removed for policy violations are not restored. Business owners generally cannot see a private explanation of every moderation signal, so avoid guessing about a single device, word, link or network as the definitive cause.

Read the complete Google review policy guide before treating a missing review as a technical error.

3. Business Profiles were recently merged

When eligible duplicate profiles are merged, reviews from both can take a few days to display together in Search and Maps. Confirm the merge is complete and that you are checking the surviving profile, not an old URL or cached result.

4. The reviewer used older software

Google specifically recommends that customers using older phones or software update the Google Maps app. If a customer reports a genuine technical problem, you can share that neutral troubleshooting step. Do not take over the customer's account, request a password or tell the person what rating or wording to submit.

5. User-created content is temporarily restricted

Google may temporarily disable reviews or other user-created content for certain profiles or categories to protect owners and users. Some school categories have longer-term review restrictions. A business should not change its primary category merely to regain reviews; categories need to describe the real business accurately.

6. Reviews changed after profile reinstatement

Google's help documentation acknowledges that reviews may be removed after a Business Profile is reinstated and directs affected owners to support. Keep the reinstatement case reference and note the count before and after reinstatement if that information is available.

A careful troubleshooting sequence

  1. Confirm the correct profile. Match the public business name, address or service area, website and Maps URL.
  2. Check Search and Maps. Record whether the same review count and individual reviews appear in both places.
  3. Define the time window. Note when the review was submitted or disappeared. Google's own wording is “a few days,” not an exact guaranteed deadline.
  4. Look for profile events. Record recent merges, reinstatements, ownership changes or category changes without making unnecessary edits.
  5. Review the content rules. Paid, incentivized, duplicated or non-experience-based reviews should not be pursued for restoration.
  6. Preserve useful evidence. Keep legitimate email notifications, dates, reviewer display names and screenshots. Avoid collecting unnecessary customer information.
  7. Contact official support when appropriate. Give Google a concise account of the pattern and the relevant profile details rather than unsupported theories.

What to prepare for Google support

A clear report is easier to assess than a long emotional narrative. Prepare only information that relates to the missing-review issue:

  • The exact public Google Business Profile or Maps URL
  • The Business Profile ID if available to an authorized manager
  • The approximate number of reviews affected
  • Whether new reviews are still appearing
  • The date range when reviews disappeared or stopped appearing
  • Reviewer display names, where legitimately known
  • Screenshots or review-notification emails, if available
  • Relevant merge, suspension or reinstatement case numbers

Do not send customer passwords, payment details, private medical information or unrelated records. If an agency is helping, the business should understand what will be submitted and who has access.

Actions that can make the situation worse

  • Do not purchase replacement reviews or ask people without a real experience to compensate for the missing count.
  • Do not ask the same customer to post repeated copies while the original may still be processing.
  • Do not promise staff, customers or clients that Google will restore a review by a particular date.
  • Do not repeatedly edit core profile information unless the information is genuinely inaccurate.
  • Do not rely on a third party that claims private access to Google's moderation systems or guarantees restoration.
Keep review acquisition genuine

A missing-review problem is not solved by manufacturing volume. Continue using a consistent, incentive-free process that invites eligible customers without screening for sentiment. See how to request authentic reviews.

Questions about missing Google reviews

How long does a Google review take to appear?

Google does not promise one universal publication time. Its current help page says policy checks can sometimes take a few days. Longer delays should be assessed using the pattern and profile history.

Why can the reviewer see the review but other people cannot?

That can happen while publication is delayed or if the contribution is not being displayed publicly. The reviewer's view alone does not confirm that the review has passed policy checks.

Can a business owner restore a missing review?

A business owner cannot directly publish or restore customer review content. If a legitimate review remains missing after reasonable processing time, an authorized profile manager can contact Google support with the relevant facts. Google controls the outcome.

Are missing reviews always fake or policy violations?

No. Google lists several other explanations, including processing delays, profile merges, older software and temporary restrictions. Conversely, a business's belief that a review is genuine does not guarantee it will be published.

Why did the rating change when the review count did not?

Displayed ratings and counts may update at different times, and a displayed rating is rounded. Capture both values and recheck before concluding that a specific review was added or removed. Use the rating calculator only for transparent estimates, not as proof of Google's underlying calculation.

Should the customer rewrite the review?

Do not coach a customer to evade moderation or dictate sentiment. If the person encountered an app problem, neutral technical advice such as updating Maps may help. Otherwise, allow processing and use official support rather than repeatedly recreating content.
