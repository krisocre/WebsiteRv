          <p class="eyebrow">Policy-based review reporting</p>
          <h1>How to report a fake or policy-violating Google review</h1>
          <p class="lede">
            A negative opinion is not automatically removable. Start with the
            platform rule, preserve relevant evidence, use the official
            reporting path, and prepare a professional public response.
          </p>
          <div class="meta-row">
            <span>By <a href="editorial-policy.html#authorship" rel="author">ReviewsBoost Editorial Team</a></span
            ><span
              >Published
              <time datetime="2026-09-02">September 2, 2026</time></span
            ><span
              >Updated
              <time datetime="2026-09-05">September 5, 2026</time></span
            ><span>11-minute read</span>
          </div>
        </div>
      </header>
      <div class="container article-shell">
        <article class="prose">
          <div class="callout warning">
            <strong>Important:</strong> Google controls its platform and makes
            the final moderation decision. No business or third party can
            honestly promise that a particular review will disappear. Interfaces,
            appeal options and response times change from time to time.
          </div>
          <h2 id="qualify">
            What kinds of Google reviews may qualify for removal?
          </h2>
          <p>
            Use the policy category that best fits the content. A precise report
            is more useful than saying only that a review is “fake” or “unfair.”
            Google’s published Maps policies include several relevant
            categories.
          </p>
          <table class="comparison">
            <thead>
              <tr>
                <th>Possible policy issue</th>
                <th>Examples</th>
                <th>Useful documentation</th>
              </tr>
            </thead>
            <tbody>
              <tr>
                <td>Fake engagement</td>
                <td>
                  No genuine experience, paid content, multiple accounts used by
                  one person
                </td>
                <td>
                  Transaction searches, dates, identical patterns, known account
                  connections
                </td>
              </tr>
              <tr>
                <td>Conflict of interest</td>
                <td>
                  Current/former employee, competitor, contractor or
                  professional affiliation
                </td>
                <td>
                  Public business records, employment history,
                  correspondence—shared only through appropriate channels
                </td>
              </tr>
              <tr>
                <td>Harassment or hate speech</td>
                <td>
                  Targeted abuse, threats or attacks based on protected
                  characteristics
                </td>
                <td>Exact text, screenshots, context and dates</td>
              </tr>
              <tr>
                <td>Personal information</td>
                <td>
                  Non-public phone number, home address, financial or medical
                  information
                </td>
                <td>
                  Screenshot highlighting the exposed information; avoid
                  republishing it
                </td>
              </tr>
              <tr>
                <td>Off-topic or advertising</td>
                <td>
                  Political rant unrelated to the experience, promotion of
                  another business, external contact solicitation
                </td>
                <td>Exact irrelevant or promotional passage</td>
              </tr>
              <tr>
                <td>Repetitive or spam content</td>
                <td>Same text across locations or repeated posts</td>
                <td>URLs and screenshots showing the pattern</td>
              </tr>
            </tbody>
          </table>
          <h3>What usually does not qualify?</h3>
          <ul>
            <li>A low star rating by itself.</li>
            <li>A harsh but relevant opinion from a genuine customer.</li>
            <li>
              A factual disagreement where the platform cannot determine the
              truth from available information.
            </li>
            <li>
              A complaint about price, wait time, service or staff conduct that
              does not otherwise violate policy.
            </li>
            <li>
              A review written under a nickname when the person can still be
              connected to a real experience.
            </li>
          </ul>
          <p>
            If the review is legitimate, put the effort into a constructive
            reply and genuine service recovery instead of filing report after
            report.
          </p>

          <h2 id="verify">Step 1: verify before calling it fake</h2>
          <p>
            Search more broadly than the reviewer’s display name. Check
            customer, patient or client records only if you are authorized to do
            so. Look for alternate names, bookings made by family members, gift
            recipients, marketplace orders, delivery partners, third-party
            payers and interactions at another location. Ask front-line staff
            privately whether they recognize the incident.
          </p>
          <p>
            Do not post “You were never a customer” simply because the public
            name is absent. A categorical accusation can escalate a genuine
            complaint and may disclose how your record system works.
          </p>

          <h2 id="evidence">Step 2: preserve a clean evidence file</h2>
          <ul class="checklist">
            <li>Save the review URL when the interface provides one.</li>
            <li>
              Capture the reviewer name, rating, full text and visible date.
            </li>
            <li>
              Take a screenshot that includes the business profile and review.
            </li>
            <li>Record when the issue was discovered and who assessed it.</li>
            <li>Name the exact policy section believed to apply.</li>
            <li>Add concise supporting facts and source links.</li>
            <li>
              Store personal or confidential evidence securely; do not post it
              in a public reply.
            </li>
          </ul>
          <p>
            Keep fact and inference separate. “The same sentence appears on four
            profiles” is an observable fact. “A competitor created all four
            accounts” is an inference unless reliable evidence connects them.
          </p>

          <div class="callout"><strong>Turn those facts into a case worksheet.</strong>
            <p>Our <a href="google-review-removal-checker.html">Google review removal checker and evidence builder</a>
              matches your concern and reporting stage to an evidence checklist. Copy or download your worksheet privately,
              or start with the <a href="google-review-evidence-worksheet.txt" download>blank evidence template</a>.</p>
          </div>

          <h2 id="report">Step 3: use Google’s official reporting tools</h2>
          <p>
            From the Business Profile or Maps interface, locate the review’s
            menu and choose the option to report it. Select the closest
            available policy reason. Google also provides a review management
            tool for eligible Business Profile accounts where a user may check
            reported-review status and, when available, access an appeal path.
          </p>
          <ol>
            <li>
              Sign in with an account authorized to manage the Business Profile.
            </li>
            <li>Open the review and choose the reporting option.</li>
            <li>
              Select the most accurate reason—do not report every category.
            </li>
            <li>Save the confirmation, date and internal case note.</li>
            <li>
              Monitor status through the available profile or review management
              interface.
            </li>
          </ol>
          <p>
            Interface labels vary by device, account and product update. The
            current official starting point is
            <a
              href="https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773"
              rel="external noreferrer"
              >Google’s guide to reporting inappropriate reviews</a
            >.
          </p>

          <h2 id="appeal">Step 4: appeal or escalate only with a clear case</h2>
          <p>
            If an appeal option is offered, make it easy for a reviewer to
            understand the policy fit:
          </p>
          <div class="example">
            <strong>Concise case structure</strong>
            <p>
              <strong>Review URL:</strong> [URL]<br /><strong
                >Policy category:</strong
              >
              [exact category]<br /><strong>Relevant text or behaviour:</strong>
              [short excerpt or pattern]<br /><strong>Supporting fact:</strong>
              [verifiable fact with date/source]<br /><strong
                >Requested action:</strong
              >
              Please assess this review under the cited policy.
            </p>
          </div>
          <p>
            Leave out emotional history, threats and claims you cannot support.
            Do not ask customers or staff to mass-report the review. Keep the
            report focused, factual and proportionate.
          </p>

          <h2 id="respond">Should you respond while a report is pending?</h2>
          <p>
            Often, yes. The review may stay visible for a while, and future
            customers will see how you handle it. Keep the wording neutral and
            do not expose evidence or undermine the report.
          </p>
          <div class="example">
            <strong>When no matching record is found</strong>
            <p>
              We have not been able to match the details in this review to our
              records. We still want to understand the concern. Please contact
              [team] at [contact] with the date and location so we can
              investigate privately.
            </p>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <strong>When the account appears factually inaccurate</strong>
            <p>
              We take this feedback seriously, but our records differ from parts
              of the account posted here. To protect privacy, we will not
              discuss transaction details publicly. [Manager] is available at
              [contact] to review the matter.
            </p>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <strong>When content may involve a conflict of interest</strong>
            <p>
              We have asked the platform to review this post under its content
              policies. If the author had a genuine customer experience, please
              contact [team] at [contact] so we can investigate.
            </p>
          </div>
          <p>
            Adapt the language to verified facts. See
            <a href="respond-to-google-reviews.html"
              >30 Google review response examples</a
            >
            for other situations.
          </p>

          <h2 id="if-stays">What to do if the review stays live</h2>
          <ol>
            <li>
              <strong>Re-read the policy.</strong> The content may be negative
              but permitted.
            </li>
            <li>
              <strong>Use any available appeal once.</strong> Add material
              evidence, not a repeat of the original complaint.
            </li>
            <li>
              <strong>Improve the public reply.</strong> Make the next step
              clear and remove defensive wording.
            </li>
            <li>
              <strong>Resolve the underlying issue.</strong> If the reviewer is
              genuine, pursue service recovery privately.
            </li>
            <li>
              <strong>Build representative feedback.</strong> Invite all
              eligible real customers through a neutral, ongoing process.
            </li>
            <li>
              <strong>Monitor for a pattern.</strong> Preserve related URLs and
              dates if multiple posts suggest coordinated abuse.
            </li>
          </ol>
          <p>
            Do not try to “bury” a review with fabricated or incentivized
            feedback. Google prohibits paid reviews and rating manipulation, and
            Canada’s Competition Bureau has warned businesses against fake
            reviews and misleading marketing.
          </p>

          <h2 id="special">Special situations</h2>
          <h3>A reviewer demands money or free service</h3>
          <p>
            Preserve the exact message, do not negotiate in public, and escalate
            internally. A demand connected to changing or removing a review may
            be relevant evidence. Obtain legal or law-enforcement advice if the
            communication includes threats or extortion.
          </p>
          <h3>The review contains personal information</h3>
          <p>
            Capture only what is needed, restrict internal access and do not
            quote the private data in your response. Select the most relevant
            policy reason and use any available privacy/reporting channel.
          </p>
          <h3>A former employee posts a review</h3>
          <p>
            Google identifies current or former employment and professional
            affiliations as possible conflicts of interest. Document the
            relationship without publishing employment records or personal
            details.
          </p>
          <h3>A competitor is suspected</h3>
          <p>
            Suspicion is not proof. Look for reliable public or internal
            evidence that actually connects the reviewer to the competing
            business. Keep any public reply factual, and do not name a
            competitor without a solid basis and appropriate advice.
          </p>
          <h3>The review targets an employee by name</h3>
          <p>
            Criticism naming a public-facing employee is not necessarily
            prohibited. Harassment, threats or personal information may be.
            Support the employee, document the text and assess the precise
            policy category.
          </p>

          <h2 id="checklist">Removal assessment checklist</h2>
          <ul class="checklist">
            <li>Is there a specific published policy issue?</li>
            <li>Did we check alternate names and genuine interaction paths?</li>
            <li>Have we saved the URL, screenshot, date and exact text?</li>
            <li>Are supporting facts verifiable and legally obtained?</li>
            <li>Can we explain the issue in five concise sentences?</li>
            <li>Does the public response protect privacy?</li>
            <li>Have we recorded the report and any appeal status?</li>
          </ul>
          <p>
            For context on the scale of platform moderation—not a prediction
            for an individual case—see our
            <a href="fake-review-statistics.html#google-timeline"
              >seven-year Google review moderation dataset</a
            >. It separates claims about fake reviews from Google’s broader
            category of policy-violating review actions.
          </p>
