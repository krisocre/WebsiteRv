Can you remove a one-star Google review with no text?

A blank one-star rating can be reported if you can point to a specific Google policy violation. The score and the lack of a comment give you little evidence on their own.

By the ReviewsBoost Editorial TeamPublished November 24, 2026

When a customer leaves a comment, you can check the location, service or date they describe. A star-only rating gives you fewer details to work with. Start by checking whether you have evidence beyond the rating itself. That will help you decide whether to file a report, save the record and watch for a pattern, or respond as you would to any other customer.

What evidence supports a report?

Google’s Maps policies cover both written reviews and star ratings. An incentive for posting a rating, evidence of coordinated activity or a documented conflict of interest can give you a policy concern to investigate. Keep a separate record for each rating.

  • The name is unfamiliar. Record which locations, channels and dates you checked. Some experiences involve a phone inquiry or a family member’s booking under a different name.
  • Similar ratings appeared across locations. Save a direct link and screenshot for each rating and profile. Record when you first saw each one. Check whether the same public account appears across the listings.
  • The account may belong to an employee or competitor. Look for a dated, reliable record connecting that specific account to the relationship. A matching display name alone leaves the identity uncertain.
  • Someone asked for payment to take reviews down. Keep the original messages and matching review links. Use Google’s review extortion process when the demand is tied to the reviews.

Write down what you can show

Suppose an auto repair business finds ratings from the same public account on three of its Ontario locations. A useful note would record the three review links, branch names, screenshots and the date all three were first seen. The owner can then explain the pattern and identify any evidence of coordination. Keep the observed dates separate from any estimate of when the ratings were posted.

How do you report a rating without a comment?

  1. Save the rating. Open the specific location’s reviews and copy the individual rating link if it’s available. Keep a screenshot and the business listing link as a backup.
  2. Choose the applicable policy concern. Open Google’s Reviews Management Tool or use the report action next to the rating. Pick a reason that accurately describes the evidence.
  3. Follow the existing case. Save the date and status. If Google declines to remove it and offers a one-time appeal, explain the policy concern and point to concrete evidence. Use the appeal outline builder to organize your notes.

Google’s reporting and appeal instructions explain the available statuses. Google decides whether a rating stays on the profile.

What can you say if you choose to reply?

Use something short that works without knowing the customer’s identity. For example, “We’d like to understand what happened. Please contact the business directly and let us know which location you visited.” Keep any account, appointment or payment details private. See more response examples for unclear or disputed reviews.

How much would removing one one-star rating change your score?

In our model, a business with 100 ratings and an exact 4.5 average would move to about 4.535 if one one-star rating were removed. If that rating stayed, it would take eight new five-star ratings to meet or exceed the same exact average. At an exact 4.9, the same comparison takes 65 new five-star ratings, even though the removal raises the average by only about 0.039 points.

The closer the score is to 5, the less each new five-star rating can move the average. That’s why 65 can be a large equivalent count while the public score may still show the same one decimal place. A displayed 4.9 can also represent more than one underlying average.

If you’re considering paid help, weigh that change alongside what customers see and the evidence you have. Compare the cost of professional removal assistance and free self-reporting before deciding.

In the 20-rating example at exactly 4.8, removing the one-star rating leaves a perfect 5.0. No finite number of additional five-star ratings can reach 5.0 if the one-star rating stays.
