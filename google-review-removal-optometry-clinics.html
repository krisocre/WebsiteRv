Look at your profile through a new patient’s eyes

Picture someone arranging their first eye exam in a new neighbourhood. Two clinics have appointment times that work. A review on one profile claims the clinic no longer takes new patients, but the post is about a different branch. The reader may book the other clinic without calling your reception team.

Check each clinic’s profile for accurate hours, a working booking link and clear instructions for new patients. Confirm that the website sends visitors to the right location when your practice has several offices. When the underlying review appears to describe a different business or reveals private information, capture enough public evidence to assess it.

Triage the issue before anyone writes a reply

Start with the review’s content and what you can verify
What the review saysWhat to do first
Names another clinic as the place of careSave the link, exact wording and public details that help identify the correct location.
Shares someone else’s identifiable health informationRaise it with your privacy lead. Give ReviewsBoost a public link and a general description without repeating the information in the assessment.
Criticises an exam charge or a long waitCheck your fee and appointment communications. Use the clinic’s private process to address the concern.

The College of Optometrists of Ontario’s social media guidance tells optometrists to safeguard patient information in public forums. If you respond, use a short general message such as “We’d like to understand your concern. Please contact our office so we can discuss it privately.” Avoid confirming that the person is a patient or discussing specific care. Follow the gap between inquiries and attended exams

Keep a weekly count of new-patient inquiries, booked exams and attended exams, grouped by practice location. A profile issue may affect calls. Limited hours or a slow callback can also affect bookings, and cancellations can affect attendance. Those are three different points to check.

In an illustrative week, four additional enquiries lead to three bookings and two attended exams. At CAD $140 collected and CAD $55 in incremental cost per exam, those two visits contribute CAD $170 before overhead and tax. Use your own collections and costs. This example puts the price of professional review assistance in context without assuming new bookings or optical sales.
