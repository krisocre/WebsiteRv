What homeowners see before you send a quote

Consider a kitchen renovation. The homeowner has the budget and a short list of firms. A recent review on your Google profile says the contractor never returned a deposit. The review names a similarly named business in a different city, but the homeowner reads the first sentence and moves on. Your price, references and work samples never get a chance to enter the comparison.

Canada’s Office of Consumer Affairs suggests meeting at least three prospective contractors, comparing written quotes and checking references from recent clients. Make it easy to connect your Google profile to the same company name, service area and type of work that appear on your estimate. Consumer guidance on choosing a renovation contractor.

Use project details to sort reviews worth reporting

Start with a screenshot and the direct link. Write down which details you can independently check. The best first note is often one sentence, such as “The post names a Toronto company with a similar name, and this is our Edmonton profile.”

  • Wrong business. Look for a different business name, city, service or identifiable project reference. Publicly verifiable details help make the distinction.
  • Coordinated spam or extortion. Save related public review links and preserve any messages demanding payment for the posts to stop. Keep the original messages for your records.
  • Real job-site dispute. Check whether your staff or a subcontractor actually interacted with the reviewer. A missed date or disagreement over a change order belongs in your complaint process unless the post itself also raises a policy issue.

If a homeowner raises a genuine concern, a calm response and a path to discuss the work privately can help the next reader see how you handle problems. Keep addresses, contract details and payment records out of public comments. Measure the estimate requests you want more of

Track calls or quote requests by project type and service area. Count how many fit your scope, turn into a site visit and become signed work. A change in these numbers is more useful than counting every profile view. Note the week a review appeared alongside changes in marketing, crew availability and season so you have context for interpreting the trend.

For example, if a contractor collects CAD $12,000 on a project and spends CAD $9,200 on direct labour, materials and other job costs, it contributes CAD $2,800 before overhead and tax. Use that type of calculation to decide how much you can spend protecting the estimate pipeline. The figures are illustrative.
