Local search fundamentals

Google Business Profile and local SEO checklist for Canadian businesses

Local visibility is built from accurate business data, a useful profile, a relevant website, genuine customer signals and ongoing measurement—not one shortcut or one metadata field.

By ReviewsBoost Editorial TeamPublished September 2, 2026Updated September 3, 202613-minute read

Google explains local results in terms of relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot control where a searcher is, but you can make the business easier to understand, keep facts accurate, show genuine activity and build a website that answers local customers’ questions.

1. Establish the profile foundation

  • Claim and verify the profile through an authorized business account.
  • Use the real-world business name—do not add keywords, cities or taglines that are not part of it.
  • Choose the most specific primary category that describes the core business.
  • Add only relevant secondary categories.
  • Use an accurate address or service area according to the business model.
  • Publish a monitored local or business phone number.
  • Link to the most relevant canonical page on the official website.
  • Enter regular and special hours and update closures promptly.

How should a service-area business set its address?

Follow the current Business Profile eligibility and representation rules. If customers are not served at the business address, the profile generally should be configured as a service-area business rather than using a public storefront presentation. Virtual offices and unstaffed locations can create eligibility problems.

How many categories should a profile use?

Use the closest primary category and only additional categories that describe real lines of business. More categories are not automatically better. Recheck the available taxonomy periodically because categories can change.

2. Complete customer-facing details

Add services, products, attributes, accessibility information, appointment or ordering links, business description and other fields that are accurate for the category. Completeness helps customers decide and can improve the match between a query and the profile.

  • Services: use customer language and accurate prices or ranges where appropriate.
  • Description: explain what the business does, whom it serves and what area it serves without promotions or keyword lists.
  • Attributes: verify rather than assume accessibility, ownership, amenity and service options.
  • Links: send appointment, menu and ordering actions to the correct destination and test them on mobile.
  • Hours: use special hours for holidays and temporary exceptions rather than changing regular hours.

3. Build a locally useful website

The website should confirm the same business identity while giving users far more depth than the profile. Each real location deserves a unique page containing its name, address or service area, phone, hours, services, directions, accessibility details, staff or local context, original photos and common questions.

Avoid city doorway pages

Do not create dozens of near-identical pages that swap only the city name and funnel everyone to the same destination. Publish a location page when there is a real location or a genuinely distinct service-area resource with original value. Thin geographic pages can confuse users and search engines.

On-page local SEO checklist

  • One descriptive page title and one clear H1.
  • A unique meta description that accurately previews the page.
  • Readable URL and self-referencing canonical link.
  • Name, address/service area, phone and hours in crawlable text.
  • Useful service explanations and locally specific FAQs.
  • Internal links to related services, resources and contact steps.
  • Descriptive image alternative text where an image conveys information.
  • Fast mobile rendering, accessible controls and visible focus states.
  • LocalBusiness structured data that matches visible, verified facts.
Structured data is a description, not a ranking lever by itself. Mark up only facts visible on the page. Do not invent an address, rating, price range, phone number or opening hours to fill schema properties.

4. Maintain business information across the web

Audit major directories, industry associations, maps, social profiles and local organizations where customers genuinely expect to find the business. Correct old names, moved addresses, disconnected phone numbers and duplicate profiles. The aim is accurate customer information, not mass submission to low-quality directories.

For Canadian addresses, standardize the format internally while respecting how a platform displays it. Track French and English brand/location variants when the business officially uses both, but do not create duplicate profiles merely for language.

5. Earn and use genuine customer reviews

Reviews help customers evaluate a business and contribute to prominence. The sustainable process is to invite all eligible real customers without incentives or rating pressure. Make the link easy to use, respond thoughtfully and feed recurring themes back into operations.

  • Trigger invitations after a consistent completion event.
  • Ask for an honest review, not five stars.
  • Do not offer discounts, gifts, points or contest entries.
  • Do not screen out dissatisfied customers.
  • Respond without revealing private information.
  • Report only content with a specific policy issue.

Read how to build an authentic review request system, how to respond with practical examples, and the Canadian review policy guide.

6. Publish useful, accurate media

Add current, representative photos of the exterior, entrance, interior, team, work and products where appropriate. Original media helps customers recognize the location and understand what to expect. Avoid stock imagery that implies a facility, staff member or result that is not real.

Name and organize source files internally, but do not expect filename keywords or metadata tricks to replace good visuals. Compress images, specify dimensions to reduce layout shifts, use modern formats where practical and provide meaningful alt text on the website.

7. Keep operational details current

Use posts, updates, menus, products and service changes when they help customers. Reply to profile questions accurately. Monitor user-suggested edits. Remove expired offers. A stale profile can create real customer harm even if it still ranks.

Do Google Posts directly improve rankings?

Google does not promise a ranking increase from a posting schedule. Use posts to communicate useful, current information and improve conversion from people who already find the profile. Do not post merely to hit a frequency target.

8. Technical SEO essentials

  • HTTPS is enforced and one host version redirects to the canonical host.
  • Important pages return 200 status and error pages return appropriate 404 status where hosting permits.
  • robots.txt does not block pages or assets needed for rendering.
  • An XML sitemap lists canonical, indexable pages and is submitted in Search Console.
  • Duplicate URL variants use redirects or canonical signals.
  • Pages have unique titles, descriptions and one logical main heading.
  • JavaScript is not required to reveal essential business content.
  • Core Web Vitals and accessibility issues are measured on real devices.
  • Structured data validates and matches visible content.

9. Measure what leads to business outcomes

Area Measure Decision it supports
Search visibility Queries, impressions, clicks, pages and locations in Search Console Which pages need better answers or internal links
Profile activity Available views and customer actions Whether profile details convert interest
Website Qualified calls, forms, bookings, directions and revenue Whether traffic has business value
Reviews Invitation volume, new reviews, themes and response coverage Whether the feedback process is used and useful
Accuracy Listing errors, duplicate profiles and wrong-location events Where data governance is failing

Use tagged website URLs where appropriate and preserve privacy. Compare year over year when seasonality matters. Rank-grid screenshots can be diagnostic, but they are not a substitute for leads, bookings and customer outcomes.

Multi-location local SEO

  • Create one governed data source for names, store numbers, addresses, phones, hours, categories, URLs and review links.
  • Give each real location a unique landing page with local content and contact details.
  • Use a locator that produces crawlable links, not only a map interface.
  • Assign profile ownership to company-controlled accounts with backup access.
  • Separate brand-wide guidance from local responsibilities.
  • Monitor wrong-location reviews and customer messages.
  • Consolidate closed or duplicate profiles through supported processes.

90-day priority plan

  1. Days 1–15: verify ownership, profile identity, primary category, address/service area, phones, hours and destination URLs.
  2. Days 16–30: correct duplicates and priority directory errors; create or improve real location pages.
  3. Days 31–45: launch a genuine review request and response workflow.
  4. Days 46–60: add original photos, service details, accessibility facts and customer questions.
  5. Days 61–75: fix sitemap, canonical, indexation, performance and structured-data issues.
  6. Days 76–90: review Search Console, profile activity and conversion data; choose the next improvements from evidence.
